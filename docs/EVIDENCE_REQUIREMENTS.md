# What biochar-AD prediction needs: evidence requirements

Status: 27 September 2026. Builds on the [Sanglier admission decision](SANGLIER_2022_VALIDATION_ADMISSION.md).
Every number below is regenerated by the scripts listed at the end and is tested.

## Why this document exists

The repository has shown that no committed dataset supports cross-study prediction
of biochar effects on methane. This document turns that negative result into
concrete, checkable requirements: which measurements are missing, how long a batch
cycle must run, how many bottles an arm needs, and how a collaborating laboratory
can check a data package before sending it.

## 1. No dataset in the repository meets the minimum information

`data/requirements/minimum_information_items.json` defines 16 items; 12 are critical
for cross-study use. Curated statuses (`data/requirements/dataset_evidence.csv`) are
cross-checked against committed data in 21 places. A curated status more optimistic
than the data stops the build; a more conservative one is allowed and listed (one
case: Kozłowski dose basis is recorded in g/L but carries a documented source
conflict).

| Dataset | Critical items met (of 12) |
| --- | ---: |
| Kozłowski 2025 | 6 |
| García-Prats 2024 | 6 |
| Valentin & Białowiec 2024 | 4 |
| Sanglier 2022 | 3 |
| CDU / Wang 2026 | 2 |
| Zhang 2022 | 2 |
| Daskaloudis 2026 | 1 |
| Heitkamp 2021 | 1 |

No dataset is ready. Four items are met by **no** dataset:

1. at least three intact, QC-included reactors in every compared arm;
2. a methane unit and gas normalization stated by the source and committed;
3. a per-reactor log of every additive, feeding change or fault;
4. two or more digestion temperatures with the same material.

The two strongest datasets fail for complementary reasons. Kozłowski has reactor
trajectories that run to plateau but only one dose and only material type plus
process temperature as descriptors. García-Prats has three doses, three materials and
full descriptors but only private summary means, no reactor trajectories.

## 2. Short cycles, not the curve equation, limit kinetic inference

`scripts/analyze_sanglier_2022_kinetic_structure.py` uses the 100 Sanglier cycles
recorded for at least 13 days (7 lab/batch groups; technical-fault cycles removed).
Treatment labels are not used.

| Family | Parameters | Whole-cycle RMSE / final yield | Error at cycle end when fitted to first 6.5 d | Lowest AICc share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First order | 2 | 3.2% | 29% | 1% |
| Modified Gompertz | 3 | 3.3% | 28% (under) | 28% |
| Logistic | 3 | 3.9% | 43% (under) | 9% |
| Two-pool first order | 4 | 2.7% | 21% | 13% |
| Gompertz + linear background | 4 | 1.8% | 28% (over) | 49% |

Values are medians over all long cycles.

- **Structure is adequate for description.** Every family fits whole long cycles
  within about 2% to 4% of the final yield. The previous status "kinetic structural
  inadequacy: plausible but unproven" is revised: as the main cause of the
  short-cycle failure it is **contradicted**. A background-production term
  (Gompertz + linear) is favoured by AICc in about half of cycles, consistent with
  carried-over solids still producing methane.
- **Identifiability is the problem.** Fitted to 6.5 days, no family predicts the
  cycle end reliably (at best 27% of cycles within 10%). The best extrapolator on
  paper, the two-pool model, hits a parameter bound in 81% of short fits, so its
  advantage is not trustworthy.
- **Longer records help steadily.** For the two-pool family the median end-of-cycle
  error falls from 20% (6.5 d) to 14% (9.5 d) and 10% (12.5 d).
- **Stop criterion.** Only 23 of 348 Sanglier cycles reach a daily increment below
  1% of cumulative yield; among long cycles the median time is 14 d and the 90th
  percentile 20 d. Independently, all 15 Kozłowski reactors reach it between day 10
  and day 16. For food waste, runs shorter than about 14 to 20 days cannot identify a
  methane potential.

## 3. Replication: bottles per arm

`scripts/analyze_design_requirements.py` estimates variance components of ln yield
from the admitted Sanglier bottles (5.0 d horizon, the disclosed deviation D1) and
converts them to design requirements. These are planning figures from one study.

| Scenario | Bottle-by-batch SD | Bottles per arm, 10% effect, 80% power |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| BRL (healthy bottles) | 6.6% | 9 with one batch; 4 with 10 repeated batches |
| LBE without the failed control | 5.0% | 6 with one batch; 4 with 8 batches (7 if between-bottle variance is at its upper 95% bound) |
| LBE including one failed control | 33% | not reachable with one batch; 83 with 8 batches |

Consequences:

- **Four bottles per arm is the hard minimum.** With three, the smallest exact
  two-sided permutation p-value is 0.10, whatever the data; with four it is 0.029.
- **One failed reactor dominates everything.** A single collapse among three
  controls raises the requirement by an order of magnitude. A pre-declared failure
  rule (failure as an outcome, with a separate survival-type analysis) is more
  valuable than more bottles.
- **Batch variance is large (SD 11% to 34%)** but cancels only if every arm is run in
  every batch. Designs that run arms in different batches or different studies
  inherit it in full, which is exactly what cross-study comparison does.
- More bottles never replace more studies: question B needs independent laboratories,
  substrates and materials.

## 4. Recommended design for the next experiment

For a batch study intended to support cross-study prediction:

1. Food-waste or OFMSW substrate, one inoculum, inoculum-only blanks and unamended
   controls, **at least 4 bottles per arm**.
2. **At least three doses** of **at least two characterized biochars**, dose in g/L
   (or %TS with the mass and volume needed to convert).
3. Run each batch **until the daily increment is below 1%** (expect 14 to 20 days
   for food waste); never truncate at a fixed short time.
4. Report methane as a stated unit with its normalization (temperature, pressure, dry
   gas) and blank handling.
5. Characterize each biochar: feedstock, pyrolysis temperature, BET surface area,
   electrical conductivity, pH, elemental composition (C, H, N, O), ash, particle size.
   Write `not_measured` rather than leaving a cell blank.
6. Log every additive, refeeding, mixing change and fault **per reactor** with time,
   amount and unit. Laboratory-wide notes without named reactors cannot be used.
7. Measure TAN and individual VFA species with units at start and end.
8. If temperature matters to the question, repeat at a second temperature with the
   same material.
9. Release the reactor-level data under a license that allows redistribution.

## 5. Checking a data package before sending it

Templates: `data/templates/reactor_observations.csv`,
`data/templates/biochar_materials.csv`, `data/templates/intervention_log.csv`.

```bash
biochar-ad check-evidence reactor_observations.csv \
  --materials biochar_materials.csv \
  --interventions intervention_log.csv \
  --declarations declarations.json
```

The report lists every item's status, the critical items still blocking, and which
items need a declaration with evidence (unit and normalization, substrate and
inoculum characterization, chemistry units, license). A declaration cannot override
an item the data can check. The command exits with status 2 on structural errors.
Running it on the repository templates reports six blocking items, as expected for a
two-day example.
