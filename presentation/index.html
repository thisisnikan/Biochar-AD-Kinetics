The problem · illustrative, not measured data

One number per curve hides the mechanism

Batch BMP studies usually report a single cumulative-methane endpoint per treatment. That endpoint carries dose, temperature, substrate, inoculum and reactor effects all at once, with no way to tell them apart.

Everything from the next slide onward is a real, reproducible number from the repository — this schematic is the only illustrative figure in the deck.
The question

Does explicitly modelling biochar dose and temperature explain and predict methane production better than a simpler baseline?

Written to fail: the hypothesis is rejected the moment held-out error stops favouring the extra complexity. The next slides show that test actually being run, on real data, more than once — including the times it lost.

Real data · Kozłowski et al. (2025)

This project's own curve family wins independently

Three transparent curve families — modified Gompertz, logistic, first-order — are fit separately per treatment and scored by leave-one-reactor-out RMSE: trained on two reactors, tested on the one it never saw.

Modified Gompertz Logistic First-order
Modified Gompertz — the same curve family this project's core kinetic engine uses — has the lowest held-out error in all four treatments. Lower amendment RMSE than the control is a smoother curve, not proof of a biochar mechanism.
Real data · Valentin & Białowiec (2024)

We tested our own hypothesis against external data — and it lost

Maximum rate (Rm)

Five independent wheat-straw-biochar doses (0–8 g/L), published kinetic parameters, strict leave-one-dose-out testing. Bars are held-out RMSE — lower is better. This project's flexible log-quadratic dose response loses to a simpler log-linear form on both targets.

This is the falsification the project is designed to surface, not hide: the current dose-response shape is not supported over this external range. It is a parameter-level challenge only — the paper's raw reactor trajectories were not obtained.
Real data · CDU / Wang (2026) thesis

A clean pyrolysis-temperature trend — that cannot be attributed to temperature alone

Six green-waste biochars (400–900°C), leave-one-biochar-out RMSE on final methane yield (Nml). A linear temperature response is five times better than assuming no temperature effect at all.

But in this six-point series, pyrolysis temperature correlates with BET surface area (r = 0.98), electrical conductivity (r = 0.99) and pH (r = 0.98). benchmark-pyrolysis-temperature reports this collinearity explicitly rather than crediting any one property — the CLI cannot and does not attribute the trend to conductivity, surface area or pH individually.
Checked, not hidden · parameter identifiability

A good fit is not the same as well-separated parameters

0.956max pairwise parameter correlation
0.95confounding flag threshold
8parameters in the global model

Every fit_global call reports max_parameter_correlation and parameter_gram_condition_number, computed from the fit's own Jacobian (Bates & Watts, 1988) — not from goodness of fit.

On the bundled synthetic demo dataset — the easiest, noise-controlled case this project has — two of the eight parameters are already confounded above the flag threshold. No real dataset here yet varies both dose and temperature with replicates, so full identifiability from real data has never even been attempted, let alone shown.

How the evidence is organised

Three tiers, kept explicitly apart

Outcome evidence · 6 entries

Real methane data that can support or challenge the model: synthetic demo, Kozłowski (2025), Zhang (2022), Valentin & Białowiec (2024), CDU/Wang (2026), and the private García-Prats CYPRUS2025 abstract.

Metadata-only resources

Material or design data with no methane outcome — e.g. García-Prats et al. (2024)'s public design/material tables. Useful for structure, never cited as evidence for or against the model.

Literature context

Published synthesis that motivates a modelling choice without being fitted data itself — currently Chiappero et al. (2022)'s meta-analysis of the dose-response literature.

This split exists so a reader never mistakes a design table, a literature summary, or a synthetic demo for outcome evidence. It is enforced in README.md and data/README.md, not just in this deck.
How mechanism claims are graded

A good fit says "predictable," not "why"

Following Pilarska (2026), every dataset is graded on what kind of evidence it actually contains for a specific mechanism (e.g. direct interspecies electron transfer) — not just whether methane went up.

Weak · Kozłowski (2025), Valentin & Białowiec (2024)

Process response only (yield, rate, lag). Consistent with a mechanism, but equally consistent with adsorption, buffering, or biodegradation of the additive.

Moderate at most · Zhang (2022), CDU / Wang (2026)

Process response plus a compatible microbial-community shift. Still not direct proof: a community shift can follow from a changed substrate too.

Strong · no dataset here yet

Would require process and microbial and direct electrochemical or molecular evidence. Nothing in this repository reaches it.

Rule enforced across the codebase: never describe a Weak- or Moderate-grade result with Strong-grade language ("proves", "confirms"). Use outcome-level language instead ("predicts", "is associated with").
Literature context · Chiappero et al. (2022)

A meta-analysis that motivates the model's shape — not its parameters

g=2.43effect on methane yield
g=2.54effect on max rate
408batch conditions, 76 studies
  • Optimal biochar profile: high ash, low carbon, high O/C, acidic pH, low surface area — sludge/manure-derived biochars tend to fit; wood-derived tend not to.
  • Dose-cost regression argues against doses above ≈0.45–0.76 g biochar/g VS on economic grounds alone.
  • The meta-analysis's own aggregated dose-response is non-monotonic: moderate doses help, excessive doses (>15–20 g/L) trend toward inhibition.
Closed-access, standard-copyright source — no table or figure is reproduced, only these cited summary statistics. This motivates the project's own non-monotonic, log-based dose term. It does not validate this project's specific fitted parameters.
Honest status

What can and cannot be claimed right now

  • Can claim: the software executes an auditable, reproducible end-to-end BMP workflow.
  • Can claim: modified Gompertz has the lowest held-out RMSE on the open reactor-level benchmark, in every treatment.
  • Can claim: an independent dataset stress-tested the dose-response hypothesis, and the negative result is reported, not hidden.
  • Can claim: every dataset here is graded for mechanism-evidence strength, and none reaches "Strong."
  • Cannot claim: that the log-quadratic dose-response form is supported by independent data — it currently is not.
  • Cannot claim: that biochar causally improves anaerobic digestion across studies.
  • Cannot claim: that the CDU pyrolysis-temperature trend is attributable to conductivity specifically — it is confounded with BET and pH by construction.
  • Cannot claim: that the 8-parameter global model's individual parameters are identifiable — even on synthetic data, two are already confounded.
A reproducible research prototype for falsifiable kinetic modelling — that description, and every number above, is maintained in docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md.

Roadmap

The next validation gate

  1. Reactor-level methane or biogas trajectories.

    Not summary endpoints or table-level parameters — the kinetic parameters need real curves.

  2. At least three biochar doses and more than one temperature.

    Valentin & Białowiec (2024) covers five doses but one temperature, at the parameter level only.

  3. Biological or reactor replicates, plus inoculum-only controls.

    To separate real treatment effects from batch-to-batch scatter.

  4. Substrate, inoculum, S/I ratio, reactor and material metadata.

    The confounders that make cross-study comparison meaningful instead of misleading.

  5. A preregistered held-out-study comparison against parsimonious baselines.

    So the model can fail publicly, not just succeed quietly — as it just did on dose response.
The ask

What would move this from summary tables to Stage 1

The CYPRUS2025 nine-biochar design already varies feedstock and pyrolysis temperature the way the roadmap calls for. What is missing to close the gap is at the reactor level, not the design level:

  • Reactor-level cumulative-methane trajectories (not just endpoint CMY), if and when they can be shared.
  • Per-reactor replicate structure, so held-out-reactor validation — the same test already run on Kozłowski (2025) — can be run here too.
  • Any microbial or electrochemical measurements already collected, even partial, to move the mechanism-evidence grade beyond Weak.
None of this changes how the existing abstract is handled: private, hash-verified, never redistributed, exactly as agreed. This is a proposal for what a next, larger data-sharing step could look like — entirely on your terms.
Take-aways

Four things worth remembering

  1. The kinetic curve family this project uses already wins independently, on real reactor data.

  2. The dose-response shape does not, yet — and that result is published in the repository, not buried.

  3. Every claim here is tiered, graded and, where possible, given a confidence interval — on purpose.

  4. The CYPRUS2025 collaboration is treated with the same rigor as every other dataset: private data stays private, and every cited number is checked against the source.

Thank you. The repository, including this deck and every underlying number, is public and open to review.
